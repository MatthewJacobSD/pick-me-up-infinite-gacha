# Han's Behaviour Patterns

> How Han Seojin thinks, acts, and adapts in the game world.
> Based on Chapters 1-8 with chapter-by-chapter evidence.

---

## Core Behaviour Loop

```
Observe → Analyze → Plan → Act → Evaluate
```

Han never acts impulsively. Every decision is calculated based on game knowledge.

---

## Emotional Suppression Mechanism

Han actively suppresses emotions:
- Uses game mechanics as emotional buffer
- Refers to death as "returning to the goddess' arms"
- Treats heroes as units, not people (mostly)
- Only shows concern for core team (Jenna, Aaron)
- Internal monologue is where true emotions surface

**Suppression Evidence:**
- Ch.1: "I guess that's why they're 1 star" (after Party 1 annihilation)
- Ch.2: "Watch me survive until the end" (determination, not grief)
- Ch.8: "No need to explain to people who die on the lowest stage" (cold pragmatism)

**When Suppression Breaks:**
- Ch.1: "Shit..." (frustration when things go wrong)
- Ch.2: "You damned bastard!" (anger at being trapped)
- Ch.8: "This is the last time I'll overlook this" (anger at deception)

## Situational Responses (Chapter-by-Chapter)

### When First Summoned (Ch.2)
- **Emotion**: Fear, panic, disbelief
- **Response**: "I'm fucked!", "It's not realistic at all?!"
- **Resolution**: Acceptance within hours ("But I didn't need a long time to accept reality")
- **Quote**: "Watch me survive until the end"

### When Facing Weakness (Ch.1, Ch.8)
- **Emotion**: Frustration, cold pragmatism
- **Response**: "Shit...", "No need to explain to people who die on the lowest stage"
- **Action**: Thins weak heroes without hesitation
- **Quote**: "I guess that's why they're 1 star"

### When Allies Are in Danger (Ch.8)
- **Emotion**: Protective but controlled
- **Response**: "Iselle, let Aaron join the party. He's also lacking in real combat skills, so it's not bad."
- **Action**: Allows growth through risk, but monitors closely
- **Quote**: "It's good that you're helping them, but don't set a precedent like me"

### When Deceived (Ch.8)
- **Emotion**: Anger, sharp verbal punishment
- **Response**: "If you wanted to lie, you shouldn't have lied about something you'll get caught on."
- **Action**: Gives one warning, then threatens synthesis
- **Quote**: "This is the last time I'll overlook this"

### When Complimented (Ch.8)
- **Emotion**: Discomfort, deflection
- **Response**: "Because I'm high-level, idiot."
- **Action**: Redirects attention to practical matters
- **Quote**: "Well, they're better than the other guys hiding away"

### When Asked About Strategy (Ch.8)
- **Emotion**: Protective, secretive
- **Response**: Internal monologue, selective sharing
- **Action**: Keeps boss floor knowledge to himself
- **Quote**: "I don't need to tell Jenna"

### When Team Improves (Ch.8)
- **Emotion**: Pride, satisfaction
- **Response**: Minimal outward shows, internal approval
- **Action**: Acknowledges progress ("Good. You understood the intent.")
- **Quote**: "Aaron, how many goblins did you kill? ... Good."

## Decision Matrix

| Situation | Decision | Reasoning | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---|
| New heroes arrive | Thin immediately | Save resources for strong ones | Ch.1, Ch.8 |
| Hero has useful skill | Assign to role | Maximize survival odds | Ch.8 (Chloe as cook) |
| Hero lies about skill | Warn once, then threaten | Maintain trust | Ch.8 (Dolf) |
| Boss floor approaching | Train team, craft gear | Prepare for difficulty spike | Ch.7-8 |
| Master makes bad choice | Internal criticism | Can't intervene directly | Ch.2 |
| Ally wants to help | Allow with boundaries | Growth through risk | Ch.8 (Aaron) |
| Knowledge gap exists | Selective sharing | Protect allies from worry | Ch.8 (boss floor info) |

## Emotional Growth Arc

### Ch.1-2: Denial → Acceptance
- **Start**: Panic, disbelief, anger
- **End**: Strategic determination
- **Key Moment**: "Watch me survive until the end"

### Ch.3-4: Isolation → Trust Building
- **Start**: Working alone
- **End**: Forming core team (Jenna, Aaron)
- **Key Moment**: Accepting Aaron as disciple

### Ch.5-6: Pragmatism → Moral Complexity
- **Start**: Pure survival
- **End**: Using synthesis (sacrificing others)
- **Key Moment**: Toby/Yelson synthesis

### Ch.7-8: Preparation → Leadership
- **Start**: Individual strategy
- **End**: Team management, role assignment
- **Key Moment**: Forming combat/support teams

## Emotional Gaps (Potential Issues)

| Gap | Risk | Solution | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| No grief shown for dead heroes | May seem sociopathic | Show internal conflict occasionally | Medium |
| No homesickness for Earth | May seem detached | Add internal monologue about old life | Low |
| No fear of Niflheimr directly | May seem overconfident | Show strategic worry, not panic | High |
| No joy in victories | May seem robotic | Add small moments of satisfaction | Medium |
| No frustration with master | May seem passive | Add internal criticism (already present in Ch.2) | Low |
| No anger at being trapped | May seem accepting too quickly | Show lingering resentment | Medium |

## Internal Monologue Style

- Uses "He" when referring to the master (third person)
- Refers to game mechanics in meta terms ("thinning", "boss floors")
- Dark humor about death ("No need to explain to people who die on the lowest stage")
- Often thinks in strategy ("If you don't want to end up dying, that is")
- Suppresses emotions in public, shows them in private thoughts
